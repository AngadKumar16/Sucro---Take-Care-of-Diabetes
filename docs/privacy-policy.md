# DiabetesCare Privacy Policy

_Last updated: October 13, 2026. Draft; host it at a public URL before submission._

DiabetesCare is a logbook for people living with diabetes. It is built so that your health data stays with you.

## What the app stores

- **Entries you log.** Glucose readings, carbs, insulin doses, activity, infusion site changes, ketone checks, day tags (sick, sports, stressed…), notes and optional site and meal photos.
- **Everyday lists.** Supplies on hand, open insulin pens or vials, questions for your doctor, foods you added or scanned, and the notes on your school card.
- **Settings.** Your name (optional), diabetes type, glucose unit, target range and alert thresholds, insulin action time, appearance, who the app is set up for (kid, teen, adult or caregiver) and which skills have been learned.
- **Grown-up passcode.** On a kid's or teen's phone, a 4-digit passcode a parent sets. Only a salted hash is kept, in the device Keychain.

All of this is stored **only on your device**, in the app's private storage, unless you turn on Family sharing (below). We have no servers and no account system, and we never receive a copy.

## Family sharing (optional, off by default)

If you choose **Settings › Family › Share with Family**, DiabetesCare uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) to share your log with the family members you invite:

- **What's shared:** glucose, carbs, insulin, activity and site changes from the last 30 days; your first name, stage (kid, teen, adult), glucose ranges and learned skills; and "I need help" taps from a kid's phone. Notes are shared only at the Kid stage. **Photos, ketone checks, day tags and supplies are never shared.**
- **Replies:** a family member can answer an "I need help" tap with a short reply ("On my way!"), which is saved in the same shared iCloud space so the kid's phone can show it. The app never lets a family member change your log.
- **Where it goes:** from your private iCloud database to the iCloud accounts of the people you invite. It does not pass through any server of ours, and we cannot see it. Your readings, carbs, insulin and notes are end-to-end encrypted in iCloud, so only the people you invite can read them, not us and not Apple.
- **Who sees it:** only the people you invite in the iCloud sharing screen. You can see exactly what they see with **See What Family Sees**.
- **Stopping:** **Stop Sharing** removes the shared data from iCloud. On a kid's or teen's phone, stopping needs the grown-up passcode.

A parent's phone that follows someone keeps that person's shared data in a separate store on the parent's device and uses it only to show it and to send alerts there.

## Apple Health

If you allow it, DiabetesCare:

- **writes** the glucose, carbs, insulin and activity you log to Apple Health, and
- **reads** blood glucose from Apple Health, for example readings your CGM app saves there, and
- **reads** steps, workouts, sleep and cycle days, only if you turn on **Activity & Sleep** in Trends, to show them next to your glucose.

Health data is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared with anyone. You can change these permissions any time in Settings › Health › Data Access & Devices › DiabetesCare.

## Backups and exports

- Daily backups are saved in the app's Documents folder on your device. You can see them in the Files app.
- When you choose **Back Up Now** or **Export**, you pick where the file goes (Files, iCloud Drive, AirDrop, Mail and so on). Once it leaves the app, it follows the privacy terms of wherever you send it.

## Food lookup

- Carb lookup searches a food list that ships inside the app (from USDA FoodData Central). Searching sends nothing anywhere.
- **Barcode scanning is off until you turn it on.** When it's on and you scan or type a barcode, the app sends **only that barcode number** to Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org), a free, open database of packaged foods, to get the product's carbs. Nothing about you or your log is sent with it. The camera image never leaves your phone.

## Widgets, Siri and Apple Watch

- Widgets, the Lock Screen countdown while treating a low, and Control Center buttons read a small summary (latest glucose, trend, active insulin estimate) that the app keeps in storage shared only with its own widgets on your device.
- Siri and Shortcuts run on your device. What you ask is handled by Apple; the app only answers with your latest reading or logs what you said.
- The Apple Watch app gets the same summary from your iPhone directly (Watch Connectivity) and sends what you log on the watch back to your iPhone. Nothing goes through a server.
- Glossary terms and guides (never your log) are added to Spotlight search on your device.
- If your iPhone has Apple Intelligence, the day's story can be reworded by Apple's on-device model. It runs on your iPhone; nothing is sent. Turn it off in Settings › Everyday.

## Notifications

Glucose alerts and reminders are scheduled on your device. Their content is never sent to us.

## What we don't do

- No analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs.
- No collection of identifiers, location, contacts or usage data.
- No data shared with third parties. (The optional barcode lookup sends a product's barcode number, nothing about you.)

## Deleting your data

Settings › Clear All Data deletes everything the app stores on this device. Deleting the app also deletes its data. Data already written to Apple Health stays there until you delete it in the Health app.

## Children

DiabetesCare is built to be used by families, including children under 13. We collect nothing, from adults or children. A kid's data stays on the kid's device, and is shared only with family members a parent invites through iCloud, never with us or anyone else. Risky settings and stopping family sharing on a kid's phone need the grown-up passcode.
